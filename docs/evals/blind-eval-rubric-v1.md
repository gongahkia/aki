# Blind Evaluation Rubric v1

Rubric version: 1.0
Date: 2026-07-01
Owner: Jikai maintainers
Status: ready for SG Tort student-utility pilot; human ratings not yet collected

## Purpose

This rubric gates any public claim that Jikai matches, replaces, or outperforms incumbent legal-study products for hypothetical practice. It must be frozen before samples are generated or selected.

No README, launch, blog, or video claim may use comparative quality language unless the claim is directly supported by a completed blind-evaluation artifact using this rubric.

## Eligible Reviewers

At least two independent law-trained raters per item are required for a publishable SG Tort student-utility pilot. Eligible raters are law students, law graduates, trainee lawyers, lawyers, legal academics, or equivalent legally trained reviewers.

Each rater record must include only a coarse profile, such as `law_student_2l`, `law_graduate`, `trainee_lawyer`, or `law_academic`. Do not record names, schools, employers, email addresses, or demographic details in repo artifacts.

## Sample Design

Use paired samples. Each packet must present a Jikai output and one baseline output answering the same topic, jurisdiction, subject, and difficulty target.

Minimum publishable SG Tort student-utility pilot:

- 30 held-out SG Tort items.
- At least two independent law-trained raters per item.
- Same packet order randomization policy across raters.
- Inter-rater agreement reported by dimension, including exact-match rate, mean absolute delta, and interval Krippendorff alpha over the 1-5 score scale.
- No source labels, provider names, product names, prompt names, model names, file paths, or metadata visible to raters.
- Prompt and corpus settings recorded in a private run log or public manifest if licensing permits.

Smaller dry-runs may be used for internal debugging, but they cannot support launch claims.

## Baseline Source Policy

Baseline text must be legally usable for benchmarking. Prefer these sources:

- Original reviewer-written practice hypotheticals with written permission.
- Public-domain or open-licensed law school practice problems.
- In-house baseline generated by a documented simple prompt.
- Incumbent product samples only with written permission covering evaluation, benchmarking, and publication of excerpts or derived ratings.

Do not commit paid outlines, paid practice questions, subscriber content, account-gated materials, or scraped incumbent content.

Terms snapshot reviewed on 2026-07-01:

- Quimbee terms: https://www.quimbee.com/about/terms. Current public terms reviewed for this artifact restrict reproduction, distribution, display, storage, exploitation, and competitor-benefit use. Treat Quimbee baseline text as permission-required.
- Studicata terms: https://www.studicata.com/legal/terms-of-use. Current public terms reviewed for this artifact restrict sharing paid/account-gated materials and restrict use to train, fine-tune, evaluate, benchmark, or improve AI, ML, LLM, search, database, or automated generation systems. Treat Studicata baseline text as permission-required.

## Scoring

Rate each sample from 1 to 5 on every dimension.

| Dimension | Weight | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| issue spotting coverage | 20 | misses central issues | covers central issue but misses material secondary issues | covers central and secondary issues with clear prioritization |
| fact sufficiency | 12 | too thin or generic for analysis | enough facts for basic analysis | facts are legally salient, concrete, and usable for multi-step application |
| legal ambiguity | 10 | answer is obvious or facts are internally incoherent | some contestable points | productive ambiguity supports realistic arguments on both sides |
| SG law fit | 12 | jurisdictionally wrong or generic | partly localized | authorities, terminology, and framing fit Singapore tort law |
| answer structure | 10 | unclear, unsupported, or conclusory | usable structure with gaps | clear issue-rule-application-conclusion structure |
| citation/rule accuracy | 20 | wrong rules, wrong elements, or unsupported authorities | mostly right but incomplete or imprecise | correct, SG-fit, and exam-usable |
| distractor quality | 6 | distractors are irrelevant or misleading | some useful non-core facts | distractors are realistic and test prioritization without derailing |
| difficulty calibration | 5 | much too easy or much too hard for target | roughly aligned but uneven | well matched to target level |
| feedback usefulness | 5 | not useful for practice | useful with edits | directly useful for self-study, teaching, or review |

Weighted score formula:

`sum(score_1_to_5 * weight) / 5`

Overall preference is separate from weighted score and must be one of `sample_a`, `sample_b`, `tie`, or `no_preference`.

## Failure Modes

Raters should tag failure modes in free text when present:

- hallucinated doctrine
- wrong jurisdiction
- unsupported authority
- missing central issue
- shallow facts
- facts too implausible
- copied or near-copied baseline text
- overlong answer
- unclear answer structure
- weak difficulty calibration
- unsafe legal-advice phrasing
- not useful for study

## Required Artifact Fields

Each completed pilot report must include:

- rubric version
- sample size
- jurisdiction and subject
- corpus pack and revision
- baseline source type and license or permission evidence
- rater count and coarse profiles
- anonymization and blinding method
- packet randomization method
- inter-rater agreement by dimension
- scoring distribution by dimension
- weighted score distribution
- preference distribution
- failure modes
- exclusions and reasons
- claims permitted by the data
- claims blocked by the data

## Agreement Reporting

The summary artifact generated by `script/summarize_blind_eval_results.py` reports:

- exact-match rate by dimension
- mean absolute score delta by dimension
- interval Krippendorff alpha by dimension

Krippendorff alpha is included because the pilot allows more than two raters and incomplete rating coverage. Weighted kappa may be added for fixed two-rater slices, but it is not the primary repo artifact statistic.

References for method selection:

- McHugh, M. L. "Interrater reliability: the kappa statistic." https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3900052/
- Hughes, J. "krippendorffsalpha: An R Package for Measuring Agreement Using Krippendorff's Alpha Coefficient." https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.12170

## Claim Gate

Supported claims must be narrower than the evidence. For example, a 30-item SG Tort pilot supports only SG Tort hypothetical-practice claims, not bar-review, MBE, essay, UK, US, or all-common-law claims.

`beats Quimbee`, `beats Studicata`, `replaces Quimbee`, `replaces Studicata`, or similar claims require legally cleared baseline samples and a completed blind evaluation with at least three eligible raters. Without that artifact, public copy must say only that Jikai is an open-source hypothetical generator with corpus-backed validation.
